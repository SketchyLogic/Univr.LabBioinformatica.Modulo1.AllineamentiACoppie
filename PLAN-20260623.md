## 1. One-paragraph summary

The lecture introduces **pairwise sequence alignment** as a foundational bioinformatics operation: comparing two sequences (DNA or protein) to maximize **identity** and **conservation** and thereby assess **similarity** and the possibility of **homology** (shared common ancestor). It motivates why protein sequences are often more informative than DNA, defines the key vocabulary (identity / conservation / similarity / homology, and the two flavours of homology — **orthologs** vs **paralogs**), then builds up the methods: first the visual **dot plot** (dot matrix) with **sliding-window** filtering and **scoring matrices**, then the **dynamic-programming** approach of **Needleman–Wunsch** (1970) for a globally optimal alignment, closing with the online EMBOSS `needle` tool at EBI and a worked β/α-globin alignment.
